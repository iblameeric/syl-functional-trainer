SYL TS 2.0 Trolleys: 2:1 Functional Trainer Setup

Four SYL TS 2.0 trolleys, a 6-post rack and one cable loop per side give you a 2:1 functional trainer, a 1:1 lat pulldown and a Smith machine, with nothing to re-rig when you switch.

The build

Lots of people buy SYL's TS 2.0 trolleys and aren't sure how to set them up as a full cable machine. This is the setup on my Rep PR-4000 6-post rack, written up step by step.

What you're building

Two trolleys are cable stations you pull from, and two are weight carriages you load with plates. One cable per side links each cable station to its weight carriage.

This guide is based on a working build: a Rep Fitness PR-4000 6-post rack (75 mm uprights, 5/8" holes, 41" between uprights) with four TS 2.0 trolleys. The same idea works on other 6-post racks with matching trolleys.

What you need

A 6-post rack, four TS 2.0 trolleys with accessory tabs, a rack-mounted double pulley for each trolley, and cable you make yourself. SYL makes the trolley for 50x50, 50x76, 60x60, 70x70, 76x76 and 80x80 mm uprights, per the TS 2.0 Trolley page, so order the size that matches yours.

How 2:1 works

At 2:1, the handle feels half the weight on the stack but travels twice as far. That long travel is what lets you do full-range cable work from a short rack. The lat pulldown stays at 1:1, so it feels the full weight. Commercial trainers use the same split: Rogue's FML-HR runs 2:1 for the functional trainer and 1:1 for the lat pulldown and low row.

Worked example with 100 lb loaded on the rear trolley:

Handle (2:1):   100 lb / 2 = 50 lb felt at the handle
                1 in of stack travel = 2 in of handle travel
Lat bar (1:1):  100 lb / 1 = 100 lb felt at the bar

The trolley's own weight counts as load too. Weigh one empty rear trolley with its horns and add that number to every plate total.

One trade-off: at 2:1 the most a handle can give you is half of what the stack holds. If you want 80 lb at each handle, you need 160 lb on that side's stack, so make sure the horns and trolley are rated for it.

Cable routing

Each side is one continuous cable. It starts at the handle and ends clipped to an eye bolt on the bottom of the front trolley. The rear trolley rides in a loop of that cable, which is what makes it 2:1.

Because both ends of the cable attach to the front trolley, you can move the front trolley to any height without the cable going slack or tight, and without removing anything. The rear trolleys only move when you pull a handle or the lat bar. The lat side adds a floating pulley in the bottom run, plus a second set of top pulleys for the lat cable (steps 11 to 15 below).

Step-by-step setup

Do one side completely, test it, then copy it on the other side. Numbers match the diagram above.

Mount the hardware

  1. Bolt Pulley A on top of each front upright and Pulley B on top of each middle upright.
  2. Mount pulley 1 under the top side crossmember, next to the middle upright.
  3. Mount pulleys 2 and 3 on the bottom side crossmember: pulley 2 near the middle upright, pulley 3 near the front upright.
  4. Fit a double pulley attachment to all four trolleys.
  5. Add an eye bolt to the bottom of each front trolley. This is where the cable ends.
  6. Put the weight horns on the two rear trolleys.

Route the cable (one continuous cable per side)

  1. Start at the handle end: crimp a ball stop on the cable, then feed it into the front trolley's double pulley head and up to Pulley A.
  2. Run it back along the top to Pulley B, then down and under the pulley on the rear trolley.
  3. Bring it back up to pulley 1, down to pulley 2, forward along the bottom to pulley 3, and up to the eye bolt on the front trolley.
  4. Pull out the slack, mark the length, then crimp an end fitting and clip it to the eye bolt with a snap shackle.

Lat pulldown side only

  1. Use a double pulley bracket on the lat side's front upright: one pulley carries the loop, the other the lat cable. SYL doesn't make a double version of Pulley A, so you need a custom dual-pulley bracket built for the load. Don't improvise one by bolting a second pulley onto a single bracket: it isn't designed for the extra load, and a failure up there drops plates.
  2. Mount a second Pulley B on top of the lat side's top crossmember, between the uprights.
  3. Split pulley 2 into two bottom pulleys a short way apart, near the middle upright. The loop runs down to the first, up around a floating pulley, then down to the second before running forward to pulley 3.
  4. Route the lat cable: lat bar, then the lat arm's tip pulley, then the second pulley on the front double Pulley A, back along the top to the second Pulley B, and down to the floating pulley's top swivel.
  5. The floating pulley hangs on two strands of the loop, so the lat bar feels the full plate weight: 1:1. The handles on that side still work at 2:1.

Test before loading

  1. Move the front trolley through its full height range. The cable should stay snug the whole way.
  2. Pull the handle through a full rep with no plates, then with one small plate, and watch every pulley for rub or a jumped groove. On the lat side, test the lat bar the same way.

Switching to Smith machine mode

The front trolleys double as the Smith machine: the barbell seats live on them, and the rear trolleys stay dedicated weight carriages. Keeping the jobs split this way means the weight stacks never have to be unloaded or re-rigged to use the bar.

  1. Set both front trolleys at the same height and pin them.
  2. Seat the barbell in the barbell seats and lock it with mag pins.
  3. To switch back, remove the bar.

Nothing needs disconnecting. Both ends of each 2:1 cable attach to the front trolley, so the cables simply ride along as the trolley moves and don't affect the bar.

Safety checks and troubleshooting

Check these before the first loaded rep, and again whenever you change the setup.

  • Rack bolted to the floor or otherwise braced: cable pulls tip unanchored racks
  • Front trolley locked with its hitch pin before every set
  • Cable seated in every pulley groove, with no rub against steel
  • Carabiners fully closed and rated for the load
  • Start light: run each exercise empty, then with a small plate, before loading up
  • Inspect cables for fraying or flat spots each week; replace at the first broken strand
